Where the memory sits

The poll is not evenly spread across the decades. It has a distinct bump, and the bump is not where the music is oldest or newest — it is where the voters were young. Bars in the shaded years — the releases KCMU's charts could corroborate at all — are split to show how much of that stretch the station's own charts actually confirm.

How this was matched, and what it misses

  • The denominator is not 739. The charts cover January 1995 to July 2000, so only albums released from 1994 onward had any opportunity to appear. Scoring against the whole poll would understate the hit rate; scoring against the eligible window is the honest comparison.
  • 31% is a floor, not a ceiling. Matching is exact after normalising case, leading articles, ampersands and punctuation. A reissue under a different title, or a single charting instead of its album, does not match.
  • 361 rows cannot match at all. The year-end Top 90.3 pages print four columns where the parser reads three, so their artist field has the song title glued onto it. Those rows are excluded by construction, which can only push the true figure up.
  • The poll data is not republished here. The chart data is a factual record from public archives and is CC0. The poll is KEXP's own listener vote, on different footing, so this repository carries the derived finding — every row of which already appears in the chart data — and not the ballot.
